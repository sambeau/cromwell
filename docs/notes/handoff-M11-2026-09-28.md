## Where it stands

Decisions are a proper document type, and agents are told them.

- **A decision has a template**, a `DEC-nnn` number, and an owner: the project
  or an initiative. A person accepts it, in the web UI or relayed from chat
  with the existing `relay_verdict`. No agent reviewer comments first.
- **Only its ruling and one-line reason are pushed**, from two front-matter
  fields, `ruling:` and `reason:`. They are capped at validation (75 and 25
  words), on every route to accepted.
- **An accepted decision is never edited.** It changes by an append-only
  amendment, a new revision a person accepts, or by a new decision that
  supersedes it. Revise on a decision now opens an amendment, on every route.
- **Every dispatch is told** the project's conventions and the accepted
  decisions on its branch of the tree: the project's, then each initiative's
  down to its own. The block goes straight after the project's name at the
  head of the user message, under caps of ten decisions and 1,500 estimated
  tokens. It shows in every transcript's "what it was told".
- **The conventions document** is its own type: one per project, capped at 300
  words and at half the token cap, accepted by a person.
- **A viewer** at `/ui/decisions`, and `create_decision`, `list_decisions` and
  `get_decision` over MCP.
- **The prompt-order defect is fixed.** Implement, code review, verify,
  estimate and authoring prompts now put shared content first and the task
  last.
- This repository's **DEC-001 to DEC-007 adopt as they are**, proved by test
  and in the demo.

The suite is green with the race detector, and the browser demo ran with no
AI provider.

## What was built

**The template and the rules (FR-1, FR-4).**

- `templates/decision/`:
  - front matter `title`, `type`, `owner`, `ruling`, `reason`, `supersedes`;
  - sections Context, Alternatives considered, and Consequences (optional);
  - `approved_by: human`, and no reviewer role.
- `templates/conventions/`: a body of at most 300 words.
- Two rule kinds, `max_words` (on a field, a section or the body) and
  `one_line` (on a field's parsed value), in `lifecycle.RuleKinds`. Loading a
  manifest checks their shape.

**One validation function (FR-3.3).** `validateDocument` in
`internal/server/decisions.go` serves Validate, Submit, the reviewer's report
and acceptance.

- A decision that revises an accepted one gets the append-only check,
  `lifecycle.ValidateDecisionRevision`:
  - the accepted body is kept (compared after parsing, so CRLF and a BOM don't
    matter);
  - only a dated `## Amendment n — … (date)` follows;
  - front matter changes only in `ruling`, `reason` and the identity lines;
  - links and placeholders are checked only in what is new.
- The one exception, **Record its ruling**, adds `ruling:` and `reason:` to a
  decision that had none, with the body unchanged.
- A new decision's `supersedes:` must name accepted decisions of the same
  owner or one below it.
- A decision without a number fails.
- The conventions are held to half the configured token cap.

**Acceptance (SD-16).** `approveDocumentAs` gains one call,
`s.onAccepted(ctx, tx, doc)`. Adoption as approved and *This was already
approved* call the same code (`acceptSurfaced`). In the transaction:

- the file's hash is checked, and validation re-run, so an edit made during
  review can't be accepted unseen;
- a per-ID advisory lock is taken, and the targets are re-read under a row
  lock;
- `surfaced_texts` records what agents will be told;
- the decisions named in `supersedes:` move to `superseded`, and
  `decision_supersessions` records each pair;
- an amendment of a superseded decision that was in review goes back to
  draft, and can't then be submitted or accepted.

**Supersession (SD-6).** The superseded decision's file stays where it is,
unchanged, and keeps its page (`SupersededDecisionAtPath`). `/ui/id/DEC-…`
leads there. Its ID is closed: adopt refuses it by `id:` line or by file name.

**Surfacing (FR-6).**

- The block is a pure function, `content.SurfacedBlock`, over the rows read
  from `surfaced_texts`. No file is read at dispatch time, so a hand edit to
  an accepted decision never reaches a prompt.
- **Admission** is nearest owner first, then most recently first accepted,
  ties to the higher number, until the count or token cap stops it.
- **Rendering** is widest-shared first, by number.
- A left-out line names at most ten.
- A title that begins with its own ID doesn't repeat it.
- The conventions' headings move one level down.
- `BackfillSurfaced` runs at server start, for decisions accepted before
  `0012`.
- Decisions and conventions no longer arrive whole as a review's background
  documents.

**Creating (FR-2, FR-5).**

- **New decision…** on `/ui/decisions`, and `create_decision`, mint the next
  number and write the template to `docs/decisions/DEC-nnn-<slug>.md` for the
  project, or to the initiative's `docs/work/…` folder. They register and
  commit it, and the UI opens the editor.
- **Supersede with a new decision** does the same with `supersedes` filled in.
- **Start the conventions document** writes `docs/conventions.md`.
- A second conventions document is refused by Start, attach and adopt.

**The viewer (FR-7).** `/ui/decisions` has a conventions panel, filters by
state and by branch, and one row per ID. Each row shows:

- the accepted revision, with an open amendment marked;
- the amendments, read from their headings, so DEC-004's and DEC-006's show;
- the surfaced line;
- what it supersedes and what superseded it;
- whether the cap leaves it out anywhere.

With a branch chosen, the page shows the block its dispatches receive and its
size. A decision's own page has a *What agents are told* panel.

**MCP.** `create_decision` (a draft, planning authoring under DEC-004),
`list_decisions` and `get_decision` (reads). `submit_for_review`'s and
`adopt_document`'s descriptions name decisions and the conventions. The
`initialize` instructions mention the three tools.

**Configuration.** `surfacing.max_tokens` (default 1,500, at least 600) and
`surfacing.max_decisions` (default 10).

## Decisions taken in the build

- **The surfaced text lives in a table**, not only in the file. Surfacing then
  reads no files, and a hand edit to an accepted decision can't reach a
  prompt.
- **The conventions share `surfaced_texts`**, with the body in `ruling` and an
  empty `reason`, rather than a table of their own.
- **A count cap joined the token cap** (R18-10). Tokens alone let long
  decisions crowd out distinct ones, and the research's line is about how many
  things the block holds.
- **A superseded decision keeps its page.** Superseded revisions otherwise
  have none (`/ui/id` sends them to the current revision). A decision retired
  by another has no current revision, and people follow links to it.
- **`reviseDocBy` refuses to write where a working copy exists** (R18-18), for
  every document type. A left-over `….rev.md` now blocks a new revision with
  a sentence that names it, rather than being overwritten.
- **Raising an issue on an approved document** now names its type. It used to
  say "This design is approved…" for every human-approved type.
- **The walkthrough's model is a local fake of the Messages API**
  (`fake_provider.py`), so the dispatch it shows is the real binary's own.
  What the provider received equals the transcript byte for byte.
- **The approval notice** for a decision or the conventions says what
  acceptance does, not "nothing starts until someone sends".

## Where the brief conflicted with an approved document, or went further

- **"A dated amendment appended to it, which is this project's own practice
  (see DEC-006's Amendment 1)."** DEC-006's Amendment 1 also changed the status
  block above it and marked decisions "*(Replaced by Amendment 1.)*" in
  place. DESIGN-010 §11 ("an accepted decision is never edited") and
  DESIGN-003 L-2 win: Subutai's amendment only appends. SPEC-018 SD-5 says so,
  and asks Sam to confirm it (choice 5).
- **Supersession** departs from DESIGN-003 §2 and §5 (SD-6): the engine
  supersedes a decision that isn't a successor, and its file isn't archived.
  Both are named, with dated notes proposed.
- **"About 150 words per decision and 1,500 tokens"** (DESIGN-010 §17b, open)
  became 100 words (75 and 25) and a count cap of ten. SD-8 argues it.
- **"Decide whether an agent reviewer comments first."** It doesn't, as for
  designs (choice 2). A project can add one in its own manifest.
- **"Give decisions a template or a verdict path."** Decisions got a template.
  SPEC-017 SD-8 had named M11 for all five untemplated types. The other four
  (note, research, report, policy) keep their path, and the M8 handoff's
  follow-up 3 stays open for them on the API route (choice 11).
- **The shared review code.** The brief said to avoid `actions.go`'s
  review-outcome handling. `approveDocumentAs` gains one call, `onAccepted`,
  beside the predecessor handling; nothing else there changed (choice 15). It
  sits in the approval, not in the review outcome.
